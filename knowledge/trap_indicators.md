# Generic trap-type indicators (general geoscience knowledge, not from the provided data)

## Structural traps
Closure is created by deformation: anticlines, fault-bounded blocks, rollovers, inversion structures.
Seismic signs: reflectors that dip away from a crest in all directions (four-way closure) or against a fault (fault closure), with reflection continuity across the closure. Reservoir properties are laterally similar across the structure, and the same sand can be correlated between wells.
Typical risks: fault seal, charge timing relative to structure formation, spill point, velocity pull-up/push-down distorting the apparent structure.

## Stratigraphic traps
Closure is created by lateral changes in rock type or by truncation: pinch-outs, channels, bars, fans, unconformity truncation, diagenetic barriers.
Seismic signs: amplitude or impedance changes that cut across reflectors, onlap/downlap/truncation terminations, channel-shaped or lobate amplitude anomalies in map view, sudden lateral loss of sand probability.
Typical risks: lateral seal (does the sand really pinch out into shale?), reservoir presence and quality, limited well control to calibrate the facies change.

## Combination traps
Many prospects combine a structural component with a stratigraphic one (e.g. a pinch-out against a structural nose). Evidence for both should be listed separately.

## Lithology and fluid indicators (qualitative)
- Low Vp/Vs together with low gamma ray usually points to clean sand; high Vp/Vs points to shale.
- Gas-bearing sand often lowers Vp/Vs and P-impedance further than the same sand with brine.
- Higher far-angle amplitude relative to near-angle can be consistent with some hydrocarbon-bearing sands, but this is not diagnostic without calibration.
